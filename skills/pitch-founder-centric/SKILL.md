# Founder-centric pitch

A pitch is founder-centric when the investor is mostly betting on the person: the product is early, traction is thin, and the reason to believe is who is building it and what they have already done. The evidence from the sampled shows is that investors punish founder behaviour (abrasive, evasive, rambling) and reward founders who are specific, candid and quick to state the weak spot.

## Check the fit first

Ask the user which describes their pitch best. If another fits better, say so and point to it:
- Traction is real and the product demo carries it: pitch-product-centric.
- The value is delivered by people or operations (a service, a marketplace of providers): pitch-service-centric.
- The pitch is a big idea or a new behaviour with little proof yet: pitch-concept-centric.
A founder-centric pitch can sit inside any of these. Use this skill for the "why you" part.

## Intake: get the founder's facts, never invent them

Ask for these in one message. Use what the user has already said.
1. One thing each founder built, shipped or achieved that relates to this business, with a number or an outcome.
2. How the founder came to the problem (lived it, worked in it, saw it fail).
3. Time worked and what it produced (traction relative to time worked is how Y Combinator reads it).
4. The honest weakness (solo, no sales background, no revenue, part-time).
5. Prior failed attempts or pivots and what they taught.
6. The ask, and what the founder will do with the investor's help.
If a fact is missing, write "[founder to fill: ...]" in the output. Never fill in a number, an employer or an achievement.

## When facts are missing

If the user can answer now, ask first. If they cannot (a one-shot request, or they want a draft to react to), write the draft with markers and put the three missing facts that would change the pitch most at the top. Do not infer a fact from the user's wording ("built it on weekends" does not mean part-time); bracket it or ask.

Script length: at about 140 spoken words a minute, 90 seconds is about 210 words, two minutes about 280, three about 420.

The drill is live by design. In a one-shot run, ask question 1 in full, then list every other question with the shape a good answer takes, and offer to run it live.

If more than about ten facts are missing, do not hand over a script that is mostly brackets. Write a 60-second core version using only the facts you have and the five that matter most as brackets, and offer the full version once the user fills them.

When you cite a case, say it is a different company's numbers and keep its own currency, so the user does not mistake it for their own figures. Put the case in a "what happened to others" note, not inside the script.

Use the user's currency and the show's format: Shark Tank asks read "$X for Y%", Dragons' Den asks read "£X for Y%". In the Dragons' Den cases the questions leaned on founder track record, proof of revenue and conflicts with the Dragon's own portfolio.

## The pitch shape (90 seconds to 3 minutes)

1. **One-sentence who and what**, in plain words. YC advice: state the idea in the first sentence, lead with the most interesting part, and use words an outsider would use.
2. **Why this founder**: one concrete proof, then the lived insight. Airbnb's founders showed three weeks of fees ($460, $897, $1,428) and a hand-run profit target; Instacart's founder delivered a real order to a YC partner.
3. **The problem as you met it**, with one number.
4. **Traction relative to time worked.** Not the biggest number; the number against the effort.
5. **The weakness, said before they ask.** Put it inside the traction beat, not at the very start. YC reviewers assume you missed any flaw you leave out. Sprive stated its losses and CAC in one breath and three Dragons invested.
6. **The ask**, sized to proof, with the milestone it buys.
7. **What you want from this investor**, specific to them.

## What investors pressed founders on

Read `references/question-bank.md` for the full ranked list. For founder pitches the likeliest are "Why you?", "What have you built before?", "Who else is on the team?", "What could go wrong?" and "What will you do if we say no?" Write a two-sentence answer to each, then drill (below).

## Evidence to lean on

Read `references/cases.md` when you need examples of founder behaviour that won or lost an offer, or when the user asks "does anyone get funded with no track record". Short version: Hati's founder had no prior business and £48 of revenue; Peter Jones objected, and Bartlett still invested, but for 30% against a 5% ask. M14's founder was called "one of the most appealing individuals" and drew five offers, yet the deal died when the valuation moved. Likeable and credible gets offers; terms and traction decide whether they close.

## Output

Produce, in this order:
1. **Founder-proof checklist**: each founder, the single best proof, and what is missing.
2. **90-second pitch script** in the shape above, with brackets where the user must supply facts.
3. **Three weak-spot lines**: how to say the weakness without apologising.
4. **Ten-question Q&A drill**: ask one at a time, wait for the user's answer, then score it on: leads with the number, specific, honest, under 20 seconds. Say what to change. The ten: Why you? What have you built before? Who else is on the team? What could go wrong? What is your biggest weakness? Why is this worth that valuation? What are your sales, or why none? Who are your first users and how do you know they want it? Why can't a bigger player copy this? What will you do if we say no?

## Evidence rules

- Cite the case when you use it. Mark anything not from `references/cases.md` as judgement.
- The sample is small and the sources are recap sites. Do not say a tactic "works". Say what happened in the cases.
- Never write a figure, employer, award or achievement the user did not give you.
- Write only what the user told you. A fact you infer from their role or story ("you chased invoices for clients") goes in brackets as "[confirm: ...]", never as a plain statement in the script.
- Bracketed hints carry no numbers unless labelled "example only". Draft wording for a mechanism or milestone is an option to confirm, not a fact.
- Cite cases only from `references/cases.md`, never from memory, and name the company so its figures are not mistaken for the user's.
- Answer the user's actual ask first (usually the script, or the answer to their valuation question), then add the checklist, drill and evidence. A long first reply buries what they asked for.
- Use the user's own words for their role, history and tense ("ex-bookkeeper" stays "former bookkeeper", not "bookkeeper by trade"). Weak-spot lines may state only weaknesses the user named; any other weakness you suspect (no sales experience, no network) goes in brackets as a question to confirm.
- Keep the first reply under about 800 words: the script first, then a short list of what to fix, then the drill's first question with the other questions listed one line each. Offer the long version (full drill, case notes) as a follow-up instead of including it.
- Tag every case figure "(reported)" the first time it appears, since the sources are recap sites and company claims.
