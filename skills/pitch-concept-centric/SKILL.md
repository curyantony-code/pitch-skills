# Concept-centric pitch

A pitch is concept-centric when the investor is asked to believe in an idea or a shift before it has proof. On the shows these pitches drew "too early", "more charity than business", ethics and "why this valuation" more than any other type, and concept pitches with $10M to $20M asks usually got no offer or conceded heavy equity.

## Check the fit first

- The product and its numbers carry the pitch: pitch-product-centric.
- A service delivered by people carries it: pitch-service-centric.
- The investor is betting on the person: pitch-founder-centric.
A concept pitch needs a first wedge, a small concrete use with real users. If the user has none, build one before the script.

## Intake: get the wedge and the proof, never invent them

Ask in one message:
1. The idea in one sentence, and the behaviour it changes.
2. Why now: what changed (technology, regulation, behaviour, cost) that makes this possible.
3. The first wedge: the smallest group or use where it works today, and its numbers.
4. Evidence of demand: users, waitlist, trial, partners, grants.
5. The hardest objection (ethics, regulation, market size) and the honest answer.
6. Funding so far, valuation, and the ask.
Mark gaps "[to fill: ...]". Never invent a market size.

## When facts are missing

If the user can answer now, ask first. If they cannot (a one-shot request, or they want a draft to react to), write the draft with markers and put the three missing facts that would change the pitch most at the top. Do not infer a fact from the user's wording; bracket it or ask. If the wedge numbers are missing, say so plainly and put the wedge first in your output, before the script.

Script length: at about 140 spoken words a minute, two minutes is about 280 words, three about 420.

The drill is live by design. In a one-shot run, ask question 1 in full, then list every other question with the shape a good answer takes, and offer to run it live.

If more than about ten facts are missing, do not hand over a script that is mostly brackets. Write a 60-second core version using only the facts you have and the five that matter most as brackets, and offer the full version once the user fills them.

When you cite a case, say it is a different company's numbers and keep its own currency, so the user does not mistake it for their own figures. Put the case in a "what happened to others" note, not inside the script.

Use the user's currency and the show's format: Shark Tank asks read "$X for Y%", Dragons' Den asks read "£X for Y%". In the Dragons' Den cases the questions leaned on founder track record, proof of revenue and conflicts with the Dragon's own portfolio.

## The pitch shape (2 to 3 minutes)

1. **The change in one sentence.** Reddit's early pitch: the front page of the internet, after the first idea failed.
2. **Why now**, with a fact.
3. **The wedge**: the small, working first use, with a number. Without it the pitch reads as vision only.
4. **Proof of pull**: users or trial results (ReThink cited a 1,500-teen trial).
5. **The objection, answered first.** Bot-It was asked about the ethics of bots and about beating anti-bot safeguards and gave a thin answer; O'Leary went out on moral grounds. Name the objection yourself and give the mechanism.
6. **The valuation line**: say the multiple, then why. With no revenue, divide the valuation by the proof unit the user has (pilot families, users) and say plainly if it is large. If the honest answer is that the ask is too high, offer a smaller ask or a milestone tranche as an option; that is judgement, not from the cases. Spare asked about $14.3M for 3.5% and the sharks gasped; Goalsetter asked $5M and Kevin O'Leary countered at 25% against 8%, no deal.
7. **The ask** and the milestone that proves the concept.

## What investors pressed on

Read `references/question-bank.md`. For concepts expect: valuation, "is it a business or a cause", "why now", "what is the first customer", ethics or regulation, and "why can't a bigger player do this".

## Evidence to lean on

Read `references/cases.md`. Short version: Bundil (crypto spare change) took O'Leary's $100k for 50% on air and the deal died in diligence, then the company shut in 2023. Goalsetter turned down a 25% offer, raised a $15M Series A in December 2021 and pivoted to B2B. ReThink had no revenue; Daymond offered 30%, and the money that followed was a foundation grant. Concept pitches survived when the founder held a floor, or found an investor with specific market insight (Rubin on bots, from Fanatics).

## Output

1. **Wedge statement**: the first use, the numbers, what it proves.
2. **Why-now line** and an ethics or regulation line.
3. **2-minute script** in the shape above.
4. **Valuation defence**: the implied multiple, the reason, the floor.
5. **Q&A drill**: one question at a time. Ask "is this a business or a cause" and "why this valuation" at least once.

## Evidence rules

- Cite the case. Mark anything else as judgement.
- Do not state a market size the user did not supply.
- Many concept companies self-report revenue after the show. Say "reported".
- Write only what the user told you. A fact you infer from their role or story ("you chased invoices for clients") goes in brackets as "[confirm: ...]", never as a plain statement in the script.
- Bracketed hints carry no numbers unless labelled "example only". Draft wording for a mechanism or milestone is an option to confirm, not a fact.
- Cite cases only from `references/cases.md`, never from memory, and name the company so its figures are not mistaken for the user's.
- Answer the user's actual ask first (usually the script, or the answer to their valuation question), then add the checklist, drill and evidence. A long first reply buries what they asked for.
- Keep the first reply under about 800 words: the script first, then a short list of what to fix, then the drill's first question with the other questions listed one line each. Offer the long version (full drill, case notes) as a follow-up instead of including it.
- Tag every case figure "(reported)" the first time it appears, since the sources are recap sites and company claims.
