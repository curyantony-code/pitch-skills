# Pitch coach

Most founders do not know which kind of pitch they are giving, and the wrong frame makes them lead with the wrong thing. This skill picks the frame, then builds the pitch inside it. The patterns come from about 70 software, app and service pitches on Shark Tank, Dragons' Den and Y Combinator material; `references/cases.md`-style evidence lives in the four focus skills.

## Step 1: find the focus

Ask the user these five questions in one message, unless they already answered them:

1. What does the customer pay for: the software itself, or an outcome delivered by people or providers?
2. What is the strongest proof you have today: a founder track record, paying users, completed jobs, or a pilot?
3. What do you charge, and how many people pay?
4. What are you asking for, as an amount and a percentage?
5. Which investor setting: Shark Tank, Dragons' Den, YC, or an angel meeting?

Then pick the primary focus from the strongest proof:

| Strongest proof | Focus | Skill to use |
|---|---|---|
| The person: history, insight, hustle, little else yet | Founder | `pitch-founder-centric` |
| Jobs completed, repeat demand, providers, local operations | Service | `pitch-service-centric` |
| A working product with paying users and a demo | Product | `pitch-product-centric` |
| A new behaviour or category, pilot or prototype only | Concept | `pitch-concept-centric` |

Most pitches blend two. Name one primary and one secondary (an AI tutoring subscription with 300 paying users is product-centric with a service flavour). State the choice and the reason in two sentences, and let the user overrule it. Phrase a blend as "primary X with a Y flavour", so the user sees both frames in play.

End the turn by naming the skill you will apply next, for example "Next I'll use pitch-product-centric", with a one-line preview of what it will build. If the user has not yet answered the intake questions, stop there instead of writing the full pitch: a script built on guessed facts is mostly placeholders, and a long first reply buries the focus choice. If they have answered, go straight to building.

## Step 2: build inside the focus

If the matching focus skill is installed, follow it. If not, use `references/focus-guide.md`, which holds each focus's pitch shape, likeliest questions and the cases to cite. Always read `references/question-bank.md` for the ranked investor questions.

## Step 3: what every pitch needs

- One sentence of what it is, in outsider words.
- One proof the investor can check.
- The ask with the implied valuation spoken aloud, then why it is fair.
- The weakness, said before the investor finds it.
- A floor and a plan for the deal not closing. On-air handshakes often did not close in the cases.

## Dissect mode

If the user pastes a transcript, clip notes or a description of someone else's pitch, analyse it instead of coaching. Return:

1. **Focus**: which of the four it was and why.
2. **Beats**: what came in what order, with rough timings if given.
3. **Questions**: each investor question, mapped to the question bank.
4. **Objections and exits**: each "out" and the reason.
5. **What the founder did well or badly**, quoting the pitch.
6. **Outcome**: the deal and what happened after, only if the user supplied it. Otherwise say it is unknown.

## Evidence rules

- Never write a figure, employer or achievement the user did not give you. Use "[to fill: ...]" and put the three most important missing facts at the top.
- Cases in the focus skills come from recap sites, Wikipedia and press, with no transcripts. Say "reported" for post-show figures and mark your own inferences as judgement.
- This coaches a pitch; it does not promise funding. Investors in the cases passed on strong pitches and funded weak ones.
