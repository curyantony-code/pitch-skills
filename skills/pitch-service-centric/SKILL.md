# Service-centric pitch

A pitch is service-centric when the customer pays for an outcome delivered by people, operations or providers, with software around it. Investors on the shows pressed hardest on cost to deliver, scalability and whether a bigger player or a lawyer could do the same.

## Check the fit first

- The software itself is the thing people pay for: pitch-product-centric.
- The investor is betting on the founder's credibility: pitch-founder-centric.
- The pitch is a new behaviour or category with no proof yet: pitch-concept-centric.
If the service is a marketplace, stay here but also read the take-rate section below.

## Intake: get the economics, never invent them

Ask for, in one message:
1. What one job costs the customer, and the alternative they would pay otherwise (the price anchor).
2. What one job costs you to deliver, split into labour, software and acquisition.
3. Take rate if you are a marketplace, and who pays it.
4. Repeat rate: how many customers come back, how often.
5. Where it works today (cities, partners) and what it would take to open the next.
6. Who could copy it: chains, incumbents, a lawyer, a freelancer.
7. Revenue, jobs completed, and the ask.
Leave "[to fill: ...]" for any missing figure. Never estimate a margin.

## When facts are missing

If the user can answer now, ask first. If they cannot (a one-shot request, or they want a draft to react to), write the draft with markers and put the three missing facts that would change the pitch most at the top. Do not infer a fact from the user's wording; bracket it or ask.

Script length: at about 140 spoken words a minute, two minutes is about 280 words, three about 420.

The drill is live by design. In a one-shot run, ask question 1 in full, then list every other question with the shape a good answer takes, and offer to run it live.

If more than about ten facts are missing, do not hand over a script that is mostly brackets. Write a 60-second core version using only the facts you have and the five that matter most as brackets, and offer the full version once the user fills them.

When you cite a case, say it is a different company's numbers and keep its own currency, so the user does not mistake it for their own figures. Put the case in a "what happened to others" note, not inside the script.

Use the user's currency and the show's format: Shark Tank asks read "$X for Y%", Dragons' Den asks read "£X for Y%". In the Dragons' Den cases the questions leaned on founder track record, proof of revenue and conflicts with the Dragon's own portfolio.

## The pitch shape (2 to 3 minutes)

1. **The job and the price gap.** HelloPrenup: $599 against about $5,000 for a lawyer. Say the anchor first.
2. **How a job runs end to end**, in three steps.
3. **Unit economics for one job**: price, cost to deliver, acquisition cost. State all three.
4. **Proof that people come back**: repeat rate or recurring base. The sampled service companies that grew had real-world demand that repeated (pools, hotels, firefighters).
5. **How it scales**: the second city or the second provider type, with numbers.
6. **Why the incumbent does not simply do this.** Hotels by Day was asked why existing booking sites did not; chains copying it was the objection.
7. **The ask and what it buys** in jobs or cities.

## Marketplace take rate

State both sides when both pay. If the user gives only a split ("providers keep 85%"), ask who pays the fee and bracket the rest. A two-sided marketplace has two acquisition costs, one for customers and one for providers; ask for both. Swimply: 20% host fee plus 10% guest fee per Wikipedia (one recap says a 25% take rate on the show), $215k revenue, $42k net profit, asked $300k for 5% ($6M). Kevin O'Leary called the valuation unreasonable and nobody offered. The company later raised a $10M Series A and a $40M round in 2021 (Wikipedia). The lesson in the data is that the valuation, not the model, cost the offer.

## What investors pressed on

Read `references/question-bank.md`. For services expect: "What does it cost to get a customer?", "How do you scale beyond one city or one provider?", "Why can't a chain or a lawyer do this?", "How much of the revenue is repeat?" and "What are your margins per job?"

## Evidence to lean on

Read `references/cases.md`. Short version: Hidrent (off-duty firefighters, $850k lifetime sales) got a 33.3% offer from Greiner and Herjavec, and the deal is unconfirmed as closed; Wedfuly (virtual wedding planning, $1M sales in 2021) took Herjavec's 10% and later sold to Wedgewood Weddings, not to him; Hello Prenup closed with O'Leary and Nirav Tolia after Kevin said he could not value it without CAC.

## Output

1. **Unit-economics card**: one job, price, cost, take rate, CAC, repeat rate, with gaps marked.
2. **Scale statement**: the second market in two sentences.
3. **2-minute script** in the shape above.
4. **Objection pre-empts** for the three likeliest: scaling, copyability, acquisition cost.
5. **Q&A drill**: one question at a time, wait for the answer, score on number first, specific, under 20 seconds.

## Evidence rules

- Cite the case. Mark anything else as judgement.
- The sources are recap sites and company claims. Say "reported" for revenue after the show.
- Never write a figure the user did not give you.
- Write only what the user told you. A fact you infer from their role or story ("you chased invoices for clients") goes in brackets as "[confirm: ...]", never as a plain statement in the script.
- Bracketed hints carry no numbers unless labelled "example only". Draft wording for a mechanism or milestone is an option to confirm, not a fact.
- Cite cases only from `references/cases.md`, never from memory, and name the company so its figures are not mistaken for the user's.
- Answer the user's actual ask first (usually the script, or the answer to their valuation question), then add the checklist, drill and evidence. A long first reply buries what they asked for.
- Keep the first reply under about 800 words: the script first, then a short list of what to fix, then the drill's first question with the other questions listed one line each. Offer the long version (full drill, case notes) as a follow-up instead of including it.
- Tag every case figure "(reported)" the first time it appears, since the sources are recap sites and company claims.
